# Weekly training volume, intensity distribution and body mass in 800 m to 10 km runners

Method note: the session's network blocked full-text fetching from PubMed, PMC and university repositories. Everything below comes from abstracts, bibliographic records and search snippets. Figures were checked against more than one record where possible, but exact effect sizes, zone definitions and anthropometric tables could not be verified in full text. Elite descriptive and observational data are labelled **[Descriptive]**, and controlled trials or meta-analyses are labelled **[Intervention]**.

## 1. What do elite and sub-elite 800 m to 10 km runners actually do (volume and intensity distribution)?

### Takeaway
World-class 5000/10,000 m runners run about 130–190 km/week in mid-preparation over 11–14 sessions, with at least 80% of volume at low intensity. Elite distance runners mostly use a **pyramidal** distribution, moving toward polarized near competition. Volume and the share of easy running fall as race distance shortens: 1500 m runners run about 120–170 km/week, and 800 m runners about 50–120 km/week with far more work at or above threshold.

### Cited Findings
- **[Descriptive, review]** Haugen, Sandbakk, Seiler & Tønnessen (2022), "The Training Characteristics of World-Class Distance Runners," *Sports Medicine – Open* 8:46, DOI 10.1186/s40798-022-00438-7. In mid-preparation, world-leading track runners (5000/10,000 m) run about 130–190 km/week and marathoners about 160–220 km/week. Both train 11–14 sessions/week, and at least 80% of running volume is low intensity across the year. Track runners race 9±3 times a year, they taper over 7–10 days, and many use altitude camps (African runners often live at 2000–2500 m). — [UiT Munin record](https://munin.uit.no/handle/10037/26245); [lida record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4078480?lng=en)
- **[Descriptive, review]** Haugen, Sandbakk, Enoksen, Seiler & Tønnessen (2021), "Crossing the Golden Training Divide: The Science and Practice of Training World-Class 800- and 1500-m Runners," *Sports Medicine* 51:1835–1854, DOI 10.1007/s40279-021-01481-2. In the preparation period, 1500 m runners run 120–170 km/week and 800 m runners 50–120 km/week. The annual split of sessions below versus at or above the anaerobic threshold is about 90/10 and 60/40. The abstract lists these in event order, which most likely means 90/10 for 1500 m and 60/40 for 800 m, but the snippet does not state the pairing explicitly. Lactate-tolerance and lactate-production intervals are regular, especially for 800 m runners. 800 m runners do more strength, power and plyometric work than 1500 m runners. The paper describes a continuum of runner types from 400–800 m to 1500–5000 m. — [UiA Brage record](https://uia.brage.unit.no/uia-xmlui/handle/11250/2789638?show=full); [fitforthem/UniPa record](https://fitforthem.unipa.it/rep:6533b870fe1ef96bd12d0703); [lida record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4087086?lng=en); [Springer DOI](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s40279-021-01481-2)
- **[Descriptive, systematic review]** Casado, González-Mohíno, González-Ravé & Foster (2022), "Training Periodization, Methods, Intensity Distribution, and Volume in Highly Trained and Elite Distance Runners: A Systematic Review," *IJSPP* (PubMed 35418513). It included 10 articles. Highly trained and elite distance runners typically use a **pyramidal** distribution, with volume falling from zone 1 to zone 2 to zone 3. Marathoners lean more pyramidal and **1500 m runners more polarized**. The authors recommend pyramidal in the preparatory and pre-competitive periods, shifting toward polarized in the competitive period. — [PubMed 35418513](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35418513/); [lida record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4076169?lng=en)
- **[Descriptive, systematic review]** Campos, Casado et al. (2022, *Int J Sports Med*), "Training-intensity distribution on middle- and long-distance runners: a systematic review." It concludes that combining high-volume low-intensity work (about 70% or more of total volume) with low-volume threshold and HIIT work (about 30%) is needed to optimise adaptations. — [lida record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4075045?lng=en)
- **[Descriptive, sub-elite]** Esteve-Lanao et al. (2005), *Med Sci Sports Exerc*. Eight well-trained sub-elite runners were monitored by heart rate for 6 months before national cross-country races of 4.175 km and 10.13 km. Zone 1 time (below VT) was 4581±979 min, against 1354±583 min in zone 2 and 487±154 min in zone 3, so about 71% / 21% / 8%. — [Bath research portal](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/how-do-endurance-runners-actually-train-relationship-with-competi/); [lida record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4009696?lng=en)

### Inferences
- The volume of low-intensity running and total volume both fall steadily from 10 km down to 800 m. The 800 m is the clear outlier: it has the lowest volume, the highest share of threshold and supra-threshold sessions, and the most strength and power work.
- In elite data, "pyramidal" versus "polarized" depends partly on season phase and event: 1500 m runners are more polarized and marathoners more pyramidal. Many elites (for example in the Norwegian model) do a lot of threshold work but still keep most volume easy. The Norwegian/Tjelta model could not be sourced in this search.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve full-text tables, such as Haugen's session-by-zone data, the Kenneally et al. 2018 review, Billat's 2001 descriptive data, or Tjelta's case studies on the Norwegian double-threshold model. None of these were reached because of network limits.
- Women and 800 m runners are under-represented. Haugen 2021 notes that the evidence leans male and toward long-distance thinking ([ProQuest record](https://www.proquest.com/docview/2561521060)).

## 2. Does weekly volume, or how it is distributed, correlate with performance?

### Takeaway
In elite and well-trained runners, observational data link total volume, especially easy volume plus tempo and short intervals, to better 5k–10k+ performance. Small trials (Esteve-Lanao 2007; Muñoz 2014) favour a large low-intensity share. Recent meta-analyses, however, find **no consistent time-trial advantage for polarized over pyramidal or threshold training**. Recreational 5k–half-marathon performance is predicted by weekly km together with BMI.

### Cited Findings
- **[Descriptive / longitudinal correlational]** Casado, Hanley, Santos-Concejero & Ruiz-Pérez (2021), *J Strength Cond Res* (PubMed 31045681). It followed 85 male (mostly Kenyan) runners over the first 7 years of their careers, with data at years 3, 5 and 7. Total distance correlated most strongly with performance, followed by easy runs, then tempo runs and short intervals. Long intervals were only weakly related (r about 0.22). Deliberate practice of easy runs, tempo runs and short intervals best predicted world-class long-distance performance. These are correlations, not causal evidence. — [PubMed 31045681](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31045681/); [Leeds Beckett eprint](https://eprints.leedsbeckett.ac.uk/id/eprint/5885/1/WorldClassLongDistanceRunningPerformancesAM-HANLEY.pdf)
- **[Descriptive / correlational, n = 8]** Esteve-Lanao et al. (2005). Total zone 1 time correlated with race time at r = −0.79 (p = 0.06) for the 4.175 km race and r = −0.97 (p = 0.008) for the 10.13 km race. The authors called for intervention studies to confirm this. — [Bath research portal](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/how-do-endurance-runners-actually-train-relationship-with-competi/)
- **[Intervention, RCT, n = 12 sub-elite, mainly 5000 m specialists]** Esteve-Lanao, Foster, Seiler & Lucía (2007), *J Strength Cond Res* 21(3):943–949. Over 5 months, the low-intensity-emphasis group (Z1) improved a 10.4 km cross-country time by −157±13 s, against −121.5±7.1 s in the moderate-intensity group (Z2), p = 0.03. Conclusion: a large share of low-intensity training over a long period is supported, provided enough high-intensity work is kept. — [UCM record](https://produccioncientifica.ucm.es/documentos/6038dab085382a6b3d7bb473?lang=en); [lida record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4030983?lng=en)
- **[Intervention, RCT, n = 30 recreational]** Muñoz et al. (2014), *IJSPP* 9(2):265–272. Over 10 weeks, the polarized group improved 10 km time by 5.0% and the between-thresholds group by 3.6%, and the difference was **not significant**. A post-hoc analysis of runners who complied best with their plan (6 polarized, 16 between-thresholds) favoured polarized training. — [Universidad Europea record](https://abacus.universidadeuropea.com/entities/publication/e453045b-50fb-4586-89a8-a3d550db3ce1); [lida record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4031233?lng=en)
- **[Intervention, RCT, n = 38 recreational]** Festa et al. (2020), *Front Sports Act Living*. Over 8 weeks with training load matched by TRIMP, polarized (77/3/20) and "focused" threshold-heavy (40/50/10) training gave similar improvements. The focused programme took about 17% less training time. Outcomes were a 2 km test and lab measures, not a 10 km race. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fspor.2019.00070)
- **[Intervention, RCT, n = 60 well-trained males]** Filipas, Bonato, Gallo & Codella (2022), *Scand J Med Sci Sports* 32(3) (PubMed 34792817). There were four 16-week arms: pyramidal, polarized, pyramidal then polarized, and polarized then pyramidal. All improved. **Pyramidal then polarized** gave the largest gains: about 1.5% on a 5 km time trial and about 3.0% on relative VO2peak. — [PubMed 34792817](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34792817/); [UNIMI AIR](https://air.unimi.it/handle/2434/884104)
- **[Intervention, n = 48 mixed endurance athletes incl. runners]** Stöggl & Sperlich (2014), *Front Physiol* 5:33, DOI 10.3389/fphys.2014.00033. Over 9 weeks, polarized training gave the largest gains, with VO2peak up about 11.7% and ramp time-to-exhaustion up about 17.4%. Threshold and high-volume training did not beat it. The HIIT group improved velocity at 4 mmol/L lactate and was the only group to lose body mass (−3.7%). — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2014.00033/full)
- **[Meta-analysis]** Rosenblat, Perrotta & Vicenzino (2019), *J Strength Cond Res*. Only 3 RCTs entered the meta-analysis. Time trials favoured polarized over threshold training (ES = −0.66, 95% CI −1.17 to −0.15). — [PubMed 29863593](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29863593/)
- **[Meta-analysis]** Silva Oliveira, Boppre & Fonseca (2024), *Sports Medicine* 54(8):2071–2095, DOI 10.1007/s40279-024-02034-z. It pooled 17 studies (n = 437). Time trials showed no difference for polarized versus other distributions (SMD −0.01, 95% CI −0.28 to 0.25, n = 221, I² = 0%). Polarized training was better for VO2peak only in highly trained athletes (SMD 0.46, 95% CI 0.10–0.82) and only in interventions under 12 weeks. — [Springer PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s40279-024-02034-z.pdf); [x-mol record](https://x-mol.com/paper/1788541240369410048)
- **[Network meta-analysis, individual participant data]** Rosenblat et al. (2025), *Sports Medicine* 55(3):655–673, DOI 10.1007/s40279-024-02149-3. It pooled 13 studies (n = 348). Using time-in-HR-zone, polarized and pyramidal training did not differ significantly for VO2max or time-trial performance. In a subgroup analysis, competitive athletes seemed to gain more VO2max from polarized training and recreational athletes more from pyramidal training, and the authors call this exploratory. — [Stirling record](https://www.stir.ac.uk/research/hub/publication/2095779); [Tübingen record](https://publikationen.uni-tuebingen.de/xmlui/handle/10900/174341)
- **[Descriptive, recreational]** Male recreational half-marathoners: race time (min) = 80.056 + 2.498×BMI − 0.594×VO2max − 0.191×weekly km (R² = 0.63), and it validated without bias. Each extra 10 km/week predicts about 1.9 min faster, and each BMI unit about 2.5 min slower. — [EXCLI J 2023](https://excli.de/excli/article/view/6198)
- **[Descriptive, recreational]** In a 5 km-to-marathon regression study (2023), weekly volume was positively associated with pace from 5 km to half marathon. In men, BMI and weekly volume were related to performance at every distance. In women, BMI was related at every distance and social variables were relevant for 10 km. — [EM-Consulte](https://www.em-consulte.com/article/1571359); [Univ. Limerick record](https://pure.ul.ie/en/publications/anthropometric-training-and-social-variables-associated-with-perf/)

### Inferences
- Across levels, more total (mostly easy) volume goes with faster 5k–10k times. But observational designs can't separate the effect of volume from talent or durability, because better runners can tolerate more.
- For the intensity distribution itself, the best current evidence (Oliveira 2024; Rosenblat 2025) puts pyramidal, polarized and threshold-heavy approaches at roughly equal value for time-trial performance. The more consistent finding is that total load and keeping some high-intensity work matter. Sequencing pyramidal then polarized (Filipas 2022) matches elite periodization practice (Casado 2022).
- Recreational runners may get as much or more from pyramidal or threshold-focused training (Festa 2020; Rosenblat 2025 subgroup), possibly for less time.

### Gaps
- I found no dose-response study specific to 10 km in recreational runners with a validated equation; the equations found are for the half marathon.
- No intervention study is specific to the 800 m or 1500 m; the trials above used 5–10 km outcomes or lab measures.
- Effect sizes for Filipas 2022 (by group) and Esteve-Lanao 2007 standardised effects were not retrievable.

## 3. How do 800 m training demands differ (speed-type versus endurance-type, anaerobic speed reserve)?

### Takeaway
The 800 m is a hybrid event, about two-thirds aerobic. Elite 800 m runners fall along a continuum from 400–800 m speed types to 800–1500 m endurance types, defined by the anaerobic speed reserve (ASR) and speed reserve ratio. Their training has lower volume, a much higher share of threshold and above-threshold sessions, lactate-tolerance work, and more strength, power and plyometrics.

### Cited Findings
- **[Descriptive]** Sandford et al. (2019), *IJSPP*, DOI 10.1123/ijspp.2018-0163, "Anaerobic speed reserve: a key component of elite male 800-m running." ASR = maximal sprint speed (from a 50 m sprint) minus predicted maximal aerobic speed (from 1500 m race time). Cluster analysis of 19 elite 800/1500 m runners using the speed reserve ratio (MSS/MAS) identified 400–800 m speed types, 800 m specialists and 800–1500 m endurance types. — [lida record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4054218?lng=en); [AUT thesis](https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/handle/10292/12094)
- **[Descriptive / commentary]** Sandford et al. (2019), *Sports Medicine*, DOI 10.1007/s40279-018-1010-5. It argues that the modern men's 800 m needs more speed than in previous eras. — [Frontiers related article](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2019.00028/pdf)
- **[Secondary summary]** The 800 m is about 66% aerobic and the 1500 m about 88%. A larger ASR tends to go with faster 800 m but slower 1500 m times. This is a coaching-education summary, not primary data. — [Coach Athletics Australia](https://coachathletics.com.au/coaching-education/the-reserve-behind-the-kick)
- **[Descriptive, review]** Haugen 2021: 800 m runners run 50–120 km/week, have a higher share of sessions at or above threshold (about 60/40 below/above), do lactate-production and tolerance intervals, and do more strength, power and plyometrics than 1500 m runners. — [lida record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4087086?lng=en); [UiA Brage](https://uia.brage.unit.no/uia-xmlui/handle/11250/2789638?show=full)

### Inferences
- Sandford's framework implies that speed-type 800 m runners, who tend to be the more muscular and powerful, are coached with less volume and more speed and anaerobic work, while endurance types train closer to 1500 m runners. This is coaching practice, not trial evidence.

### Gaps
- No RCT tests different volume or intensity prescriptions for speed-type versus endurance-type 800 m runners.
- Body mass and height of Sandford's clusters could not be retrieved.

## 4. Body mass and physique across events, and whether size moderates the response to volume or intensity

### Takeaway
Runners get lighter and smaller as events get longer, and 800 m runners are taller and heavier than 5–10 km runners. BMI or body mass predicts slower recreational 5k–half-marathon times independently of weekly km. I found **no study testing whether body mass or physique changes the performance response to volume or intensity**. The only moderation evidence concerns injury: BMI modifies the volume–injury relationship in recreational runners.

### Cited Findings
- **[Descriptive]** Among top-100 international male runners from 100 m to the marathon, body mass and BMI fall as race distance increases. Speed was significantly associated with mass and BMI, and only moderately with height. — [INSEP HAL, "BMI and performance"](https://hal-insep.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01769542/file/BMI%20a%20performance.pdf)
- **[Descriptive, national-level Indian runners]** 800 m runners were heavier and taller, with larger limb and breadth measures, than 1500–5000 m and 5000–10,000 m runners. The 5000–10,000 m runners had the highest ponderal index, meaning the most linear build. — [CORE PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/234696893.pdf)
- **[Descriptive, young elite males]** BMI did not differ significantly between middle-distance (800/1500 m) and long-distance (3000 m, steeplechase, 5000 m) runners. — [IJERPH 2020, 17:674](https://mdpi.com/1660-4601/17/2/674)
- **[Descriptive]** In elite and recreational runners, body composition predicted performance better than BMI. — [Sci Rep 2023](https://link.springer.com/10.1038/s41598-023-45064-9)
- **[Descriptive, recreational]** In the half-marathon equation, BMI (+2.498 min per unit) and weekly km (−0.191 min per km) are independent predictors. — [EXCLI J 2023](https://excli.de/excli/article/view/6198). BMI is also correlated with weekly mileage, so their effects overlap ([same source discussion](https://excli.de/excli/article/view/6198)).
- **[Prospective cohort, n = 517 recreational]** Malisoux et al. (2015): BMI modifies the relationship between training load and running-related injury. There was a negative interaction between BMI and weekly volume, and heavier runners spread their volume over more, shorter sessions. — [LIH research portal](https://researchportal.lih.lu/en/publications/a-step-towards-understanding-the-mechanisms-of-running-related-in/)
- **[Prospective, novice runners]** Obese novices had a higher injury risk if they ran more than 3 km in the first week of a programme. — [PMC4555503 (as listed in search)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4555503)
- **[Intervention]** In Stöggl & Sperlich 2014, only the HIIT group lost body mass (−3.7%). — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2014.00033/full)

### Inferences
- In practice, the heavier and more muscular profile clusters at the 800 m end, and those runners are prescribed less volume and more speed, anaerobic and strength work (Haugen 2021; Sandford 2019). This aligns event, physique and training, but it is not evidence that heavier runners respond differently to the same prescription.
- For heavier recreational runners, the evidence that BMI moderates the volume–injury link suggests building volume gradually and spreading it over more frequent, shorter sessions. This is an injury argument, not a performance-response argument.

### Gaps
- I found **no** study of body mass or physique as a moderator of performance gains from volume or intensity distribution (polarized, pyramidal, threshold or HIIT) in 800 m–10 km runners. The Rosenblat 2025 IPD analysis looked at athlete level, not body size, as a moderator, as far as the abstract shows.
- I found no peer-reviewed per-event mean height, mass or BMI for current world-class 800/1500/5000/10,000 m runners in accessible full text.
